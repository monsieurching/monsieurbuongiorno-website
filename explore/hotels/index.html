Hotels that hold a life

The ones I return to in memory for the people, the service, the art, and the moments they have held.

By Albert Ching · November 25, 2026

Hotels are more than places to stay. At their best, they are institutions that hold you and your favorite creatures through life’s most memorable moments. The very best make you want to bring a little of their spirit home.

These are mine, in no particular order. Some I have stayed in; others I have come to love over lunch, a celebration, or simply by walking through.

Waikīkī · Oʻahu

Halekulani

Halekulani has been a backdrop to my junior prom, my sister’s wedding, dear friends’ weddings, and more Christmas celebrations than I can count. My parents, who have worked in real estate with new Chinese immigrants for four decades, know many of the people who have cared for guests at Orchids for years. That continuity is what makes the place feel like an institution to me.

My ideal Sunday, or Christmas morning, begins at the buffet: coconut cake, vanilla ice cream, and coffee, with the ocean just beyond. Later, a mai tai at House Without a Key, Hawaiian music, and the sunset under the old kiawe tree. Halekulani’s name means “house befitting heaven”; the hotel has carried it since 1917.

Waikīkī · Oʻahu

The Royal Hawaiian

The Pink Palace’s color seems to meet the sky on a clear morning. Its grounds occupy a part of Waikīkī with a much older royal history, long before the hotel opened. When I was growing up a few blocks away, the Brothers Cazimero performed in its grand ballroom. I still carry the sound of those evenings with me.

Today I love it for a quieter reason. Walk through early, before the beach crowds arrive, and notice the trees, the red ginger, and the particular hush of the grounds.

Kahala · Oʻahu

The Kahala

Outside Waikīkī, The Kahala has been the setting for many family celebrations. Lunch at Plumeria Beach House, dinner at Alan Wong’s, and the short walk to see the dolphins always give the little ones something to talk about. It feels like a place made for gathering several generations at once.

San Francisco

Palace Hotel

As someone from Hawaiʻi who has lived in San Francisco for so long, I cannot walk through the Palace without thinking of King Kalākaua. He died at the original hotel in 1891. Queen Liliʻuokalani succeeded him; the overthrow of the Hawaiian Kingdom followed in 1893. The present building is later, but it still holds that connection in its memory and in the two throne chairs displayed inside.

The Garden Court is magnificent. I come for the room and the history more than for its service, which has never been the reason this hotel belongs on my list.

Antibes · France

Hôtel du Cap-Eden-Roc

I have been here only once, for lunch, and I still think about it. The property is immaculate: elegant buildings, the Mediterranean close enough to dive straight into, and service that made me feel just as welcome as the guests arriving by private boat. During Cannes, the hotel draws the glitterati. For an afternoon, it was wonderful to sit among them and dream a little.

Saint-Paul-de-Vence · France

La Colombe d’Or

Up in Saint-Paul-de-Vence is a hotel where art and hospitality have lived together for generations. Picasso, Chagall, and other artists were part of its world; their work still belongs to the rooms and gardens. Lunch here feels like stepping into a time when art was simply part of life.

Order the basket of Provençal crudités. The produce is so fresh that it reminds me why I love the markets around San Francisco Bay, even as the setting could hardly be more different.

Paris · France

Le Bristol

Le Bristol held the most unforgettable night of our lives: our wedding celebration, delayed three years by the pandemic. Through every change of plan, the people there treated us with care. When we returned for our anniversary, they still remembered details about us. That is what understated service looks like.

We brought our little dog Charlie for the celebration and his tenth birthday; I think it may have been his best week yet. The in-room breakfast and nautical-themed pool are unforgettable, but a stay is not the only way to enjoy the place. Go for lunch or tea and spend a little time with the flowers in the lobby. I still remember the alstroemeria, delphiniums, and lisianthus that greeted us.
