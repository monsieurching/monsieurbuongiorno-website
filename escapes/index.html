The Edit / Great Escapes

Five places I would return to tomorrow

From a morning drive on the Mara to a day of reading in Boston, these are the places that make the world go quiet.

By Monsieur Buongiorno · Founder and perfumer, Maison Buongiorno

A great escape gives me a way out of the noise of the world. I do not always need to go far. I need a place that changes what I notice: a sound in the dark, the color of a reef, the pleasure of doing almost nothing.

If I could return tomorrow to five places I have already been, these are the ones I would choose.

Morning in the Maasai Mara

Nothing quite matches the suspense of a morning safari drive in Kenya’s Maasai Mara. You set out hoping for lions, elephants in the distance, perhaps a cheetah on the hunt, and learn to pay attention to every movement in the grass. Even the quiet stretches feel charged with possibility.

At night, I remember listening for animals beyond the camp. Walking near a giraffe was thrilling in a completely different way. And when it was time to return to our room, we called for an escort because hippos might be out after dark. The whole landscape insisted that we were visitors.

Underwater in Fiji

I grew up in Hawaiʻi, where I knew the reefs around Hanauma Bay as a child and saw how heavily visited they had become. In Fiji, snorkeling over coral that looked so abundant and alive to me felt like a revelation. I remember layers of shape and color falling away into deep blue.

Some of the places we visited took a boat to reach. Being in the water, far from the usual bustle, made me feel briefly as though I had entered a world operating on its own time.

Dolphins and stars on Kangaroo Island

Few things make me happier than dolphins, kangaroos, and a night sky full of stars. Kangaroo Island brought all three together. I have tried to swim near dolphins off Oʻahu and Maui, but they usually vanished before we got close. Off Kangaroo Island, we were in the water as dolphins lingered nearby in the shallows. It felt like a gift, and one to meet on their terms.

Back on land, the island’s kangaroos seemed to appear at just the right hour for a local gin and tonic. Then, late at night, the Milky Way took over the sky. I lost sleep looking up and would happily do it again.

Rain in Volcano, Hawaiʻi

Near Kīlauea on the island of Hawaiʻi, the rainforest around Volcano has a quiet that I still think about. Ten years ago, my mother and I found a small place to stay. The pleasure was simple: listening to rain move through the trees and reading a book without needing to be anywhere else.

The volcano gives the landscape its own sense of time. Yet what stays with me most is the sound of water, the green outside the window, and the permission to rest.

One day at The Newbury Boston

Sometimes the best escape is only a change of room. We spent a day at The Newbury around Christmas and made almost no plans: breakfast in the room, Monocle magazine, and time to read. A morning Barry’s class and a walk by the frozen Public Garden gave the day just enough adventure.

It was close to the comforts of home, but felt apart from the ordinary rhythm of it. I would go back for precisely that feeling.
