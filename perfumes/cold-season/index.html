The perfumes I reach for in the colder season

From a meeting at the Barneys perfume counter to rain soaked woods and soft leather: the scents that taught me to love perfume beyond my own bottles.

By Albert Ching · Founder and perfumer, Maison Buongiorno

Eleven years ago, I visited the Barneys perfume counter and met Frédéric Malle. Until then, I had known perfumes mostly as finished objects. That day, I began to know the people behind them: their passions, their ideas, and the extraordinary intelligence in their work.

My first bottle from the collection was L’Eau d’Hiver by Jean-Claude Ellena. It was so restrained that I almost had to lean in. In a world of loud perfumes, its subtlety felt like a revelation.

Then came Carnal Flower by Dominique Ropion, the most convincing expression of tuberose I had experienced. Tuberose absolute gives you the flower’s heavy, indolic side, but a living blossom has a first, almost intoxicating rush that is harder to hold. To my nose, the melon freshness in Carnal Flower brings that opening moment back. It makes the flower feel alive before its deeper facets arrive.

And then there is Olivia Giacobetti’s En Passant: an unexpected, airy recreation of lilac. I had remembered it as plum, which says something about the way scent and memory slip around each other. Returning to it, I find the lilac again.

What I wear when the air turns cold

I grew up in Hawai‘i, where I was always longing for flowers. It took two decades in San Francisco and another seven years in Boston for me to truly appreciate the smell of the colder season. Now I find myself reaching for perfume that lets the weather in.

Celine

Cologne Française

I discovered this in January on Rue Saint Honoré in Paris, while we were searching for wedding venues. It reminds me of older colognes, with a softer, less citrus forward character. It carries that particular Paris day with it.

Perfumer H

Rain Wood

I found Rain Wood at Perfumer H’s London shop, near Chiltern Firehouse, with the Monocle shop next door. Elemi and the woods give me the impression of a dark forest in the rain. I don’t get the synthetic petrichor effect that can make a rain scent feel literal; this feels natural and soulful.

D’Orsay

Te Dire Oui. V.H.

Leather is a new mood for me. This one is the expensive kind: supple, polished, and easy to layer with almost anything. Saffron gives the leather a little glow.
